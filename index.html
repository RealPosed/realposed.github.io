Predictions you can check.

Some models end up deciding whether a part gets certified or a well gets drilled. A model in that position has to agree with the physics and with the measurements, and it has to say where it is unsure.

Infer the dynamics

Most systems are only partly observed: a few sensors, a coarse simulation, a satellite pass every few hours. We reconstruct the full state from those pieces and learn the operators that move it forward, including terms the textbook equations leave out.

Check the mathematics

Fitting the training data is no evidence that a model conserves energy or stays stable over a long rollout. We test each model against the structure of its equations, and prove the important properties where that is feasible.

Predict beyond the data

The next design will have a new geometry, a different boundary condition or an operating point nobody simulated. A model that keeps the physics explicit has a chance of saying something useful there.

From measurements to design decisions.

The governing equations stay part of the model from the first measurement to the final design. Conservation laws constrain what it learns, and generative methods give each forecast a spread, so you can see how confident it is.

1. Observe and encode

Hover over a step to see what it does to a single signal.

  1. 1

    Observe and encode

    Sensors, experiments, simulations and remote sensing each see part of a system, at different resolutions and with different noise. We encode them into one shared representation of the fields, the geometry and the boundary conditions.

  2. 2

    Infer the hidden state

    From those partial views, reconstruct the full physical state. This is an inverse problem, usually an ill-posed one, so the regularization has to be chosen with care.

  3. 3

    Discover the dynamics

    Learn the operator that advances the state in time. Known physics is written in directly, and the network fits only the residual, such as unresolved turbulence or an unknown constitutive law.

  4. 4

    Verify

    Check that the learned model conserves what it should, stays stable over long rollouts and is calibrated on held-out data. Where it is practical, prove the key properties as well.

  5. 5

    Simulate

    Run the model forward. Flow matching evolves the state efficiently, and diffusion samples several physically plausible futures; the spread between them is the uncertainty.

  6. 6

    Design and control

    Ask what happens with a different geometry or operating point, search for a better design, or close a control loop around the system.

Most real inverse problems are ill-posed.

In 1902 Jacques Hadamard called a problem well-posed if a solution exists, is unique, and changes continuously with the data. Inverse problems in practice tend to fail the last test: a little measurement noise can swing the answer wildly.

The panel shows a signal that was blurred and then measured with noise. The naive inverse undoes the blur exactly but amplifies the noise along with it. Each regularizer avoids this by assuming something about the answer: Tikhonov that its values are small, Wiener that it is smooth, total variation that it is flat except at a few sharp jumps, and L1 that it is mostly zero.

The research behind it.

RealPosed grew out of two groups at UCLA. Andrea Bertozzi’s group has spent three decades on nonlinear PDEs, fluid dynamics and their use in data science. Wei Wang’s group works on machine learning for dynamical systems, from graph ODEs to diffusion models for fluid simulation. The company is an attempt to put both into one pretrained model.

Bertozzi group, 1993 to 2026

  5. 2026Generative simulation. Self-guided diffusion for CFD; neural Lagrangian simulation.

The next 15 to 18 months.

  1. A working prototype

    A PDE-grounded world model that learns dynamics from data and still holds up when moved to a different physical regime.

  2. Verification you can inspect

    Every model comes with the checks that justify it, and its most important properties are proved formally where that is feasible.

  3. 10 to 100× faster on selected workloads

    At accuracy an engineer would accept, including on geometries and boundary conditions the model has not seen.

  4. Outside validation

    Benchmarks developed with national laboratories and industrial partners, so results are measured on their problems and not only on ours.

These are goals, not results we can show yet.
